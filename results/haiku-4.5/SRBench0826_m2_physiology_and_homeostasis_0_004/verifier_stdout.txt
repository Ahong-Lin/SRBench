# Glucose-Insulin Regulation: Mathematical Law Discovery

## Executive Summary

This document describes the discovery of the mathematical law governing glucose-insulin regulation in a bolus glucose clearance experiment. Through systematic symbolic regression on a training dataset of 4,500 samples, I identified the optimal model with **R² = 0.9986** (99.86% variance explained).

## The Mathematical Law

```
dG/dt = 0.38885 + 0.01586·G - 0.22769·I - 0.04736·I² - 0.29903·Ia - 0.03715·I·Ia + 0.08093·Ia²
```

Where:
- **dG/dt**: Rate of change of plasma glucose (output to predict)
- **G**: Plasma glucose concentration (input)
- **I**: Plasma insulin concentration (input)
- **Ia**: Active insulin (input)
- Coefficients fitted via least-squares regression on training data

## Model Development Process

### 1. Initial Exploration
The training dataset contained 4,500 observations with five columns: t (time), G (glucose), I (insulin), Ia (active insulin), and dG_dt (target).

**Correlation Analysis:**
- I (insulin) showed the strongest negative correlation with dG_dt: r = -0.9581
- Ia (active insulin) showed moderate negative correlation: r = -0.5314
- G (glucose) showed weak negative correlation: r = -0.4787
- This suggested insulin dynamics dominate the rate equation

### 2. Model Selection Strategy
I tested progressively more complex models:

| Model | Formula | R² | Notes |
|-------|---------|-----|-------|
| Linear | a + b·G + c·I + d·Ia | 0.9619 | Simple baseline |
| Quadratic (I) | a + b·G + c·I + d·I² + e·Ia | 0.9907 | Added I² term |
| With I·Ia | a + b·G + c·I + d·I² + e·Ia + f·I·Ia | 0.9949 | Added interaction |
| **Final (C4)** | **a + b·G + c·I + d·I² + e·Ia + f·I·Ia + h·Ia²** | **0.9986** | **Best fit** |

### 3. Why This Model Works

The discovered model captures several biological mechanisms:

#### Baseline (a = 0.389)
Positive offset representing basal glucose clearance and production rate when all concentrations are at minimal levels.

#### Linear Terms
- **b·G (coefficient = +0.0159)**: Weak positive effect—glucose slightly promotes its own clearance (direct glucose uptake or feedback)
- **c·I (coefficient = -0.2277)**: Strong negative effect—insulin suppresses glucose increase rate (promotes glucose clearance)
- **e·Ia (coefficient = -0.2990)**: Active insulin has stronger inhibitory effect than total insulin

#### Quadratic Terms
- **d·I² (coefficient = -0.0474)**: Weak negative quadratic term—captures saturation effects at high insulin levels
- **h·Ia² (coefficient = +0.0809)**: Positive quadratic in active insulin—represents feedback suppression or second-phase effects

#### Interaction Term
- **f·I·Ia (coefficient = -0.0371)**: Synergistic interaction between total and active insulin—when both are elevated, glucose clearance is further suppressed

## Biological Interpretation

### Feedback Dynamics
The coupled feedback system of glucose-insulin regulation is captured by the model structure:

1. **Glucose rise** → triggers insulin secretion
2. **Elevated insulin** → promotes glucose uptake (negative dG_dt)
3. **Glucose falls** → insulin secretion decreases
4. **Insulin clearance** → Ia decays, glucose rise resumes

The model encodes this through:
- Strong negative coefficients for I and Ia terms (insulin action suppresses dG_dt)
- Quadratic terms capturing saturation and feedback effects
- The I·Ia interaction term capturing the synergistic suppression when the system is highly activated

### Expected Behaviors
- At baseline (G=1, I=0.5, Ia=0): dG_dt ≈ +0.27 (glucose rising, little insulin action)
- At peak insulin (G=10, I=3, Ia=3.5): dG_dt ≈ -1.1 (strong glucose clearance)
- As time progresses and insulin clears: dG_dt → +0.4 (glucose returning to baseline)
