# Discovered Mathematical Law: Respiratory Pathogen Outbreak Dynamics

## Summary

The instantaneous rate of change of infected individuals (`dI_dt`) in a respiratory pathogen outbreak follows this linear relationship:

```
dI/dt = 0.0002005081 * S*I - 0.1308127623 * E + 0.0013426621 * S - 0.6231338570
```

where:
- **S** = number of susceptible individuals
- **I** = number of currently infected individuals  
- **E** = number of exposed but not yet infectious individuals

## Model Performance

- **R² Score**: 0.9606 (explains 96.06% of variance)
- **Mean Absolute Error**: ~0.33 cases per time unit
- **Standard Deviation of Residuals**: 0.348 cases per time unit

The model was discovered through systematic linear regression analysis of the training dataset containing 4,500 time-series observations from a single outbreak trajectory.

## Biological Interpretation

### Term 1: Transmission Term (0.0002005 * S*I)

This positive term represents new infections generated by contacts between susceptible and infected individuals. The coefficient 0.0002005 represents the effective transmission rate scaled by population normalization. This is the classic "law of mass action" in epidemiology:
- Increases with more susceptible individuals (more people to infect)
- Increases with more infected individuals (more sources of infection)
- The product S*I captures the frequency of S-I contacts

### Term 2: Progression Term (-0.1308 * E)

This negative term represents individuals progressing from the exposed state to infectious state. The negative coefficient indicates that a portion of the exposed pool leaves the infectious class as they recover or transition elsewhere. In SEIR framework, exposed individuals transition to infected, but the negative coefficient here suggests net flow accounting for recovery or other losses.

### Term 3: Susceptible Effect (0.0013426 * S)

This positive term represents a secondary effect of susceptible population size independent of transmission dynamics. It could reflect:
- Contact rate baseline that depends on population size
- Density-dependent transmission effects
- Behavioral or mixing pattern effects

### Term 4: Baseline Constant (-0.6231)

This constant term represents a baseline rate independent of population state. It could capture:
- Background mortality/recovery processes
- Time-dependent effects not captured by state variables
- Model normalization effects

## Discovery Process

1. **Initial Exploration**: Tested simple linear models with individual variables (S, I, E, R, t)
   - Single variables provided poor fit (R² < 0.47)

2. **Two-Variable Models**: Tested pairwise combinations
   - Best simple model: S*I + I (R² = 0.917)

3. **Three-Variable Models**: Added third predictors
   - S*I + E + t (R² = 0.953)
   - S*I + E + S (R² = 0.9606) ← **Selected**
   - S*I + E + R (R² = 0.9605)

4. **Final Selection**: Chose S*I + E + S for being:
   - Interpretable within SEIR framework
   - Highest R² among biological variable combinations
   - Lowest standard deviation of residuals (0.348)
   - Most parsimonious (uses only S, I, E)

## Model Limitations

- **Non-zero residuals**: Maximum error ~0.84 cases; some individual predictions deviate significantly from observations
- **Initialization effects**: Errors are largest at outbreak beginning when E and I are very small
- **End-phase behavior**: Predictions less accurate as outbreak wanes (small I, E)
- **Linear assumption**: May miss nonlinear transmission dynamics or density-dependent effects at extremes

## Alternative Formulations Tested

| Model | R² | Notes |
|-------|-----|-------|
| S*I only | 0.217 | Underfits |
| S*I + I | 0.917 | Good but missing E effects |
| S*I + E + I | 0.943 | Similar performance |
| S*I + E + t | 0.953 | Better, but includes time |
| S*I + E + R | 0.9605 | Equivalent to selected model |
| S*I + E + S | 0.9606 | **Selected** |

## Epidemiological Context

This outbreak occurs in a fully susceptible population with:
- No birth/immigration events
- No waning immunity (recovered individuals stay immune)
- 1000 total individuals
- Initial condition: ~1 infected, rest susceptible

The discovered law describes the dynamics during the entire outbreak lifecycle, from early exponential growth through peak infections to eventual decline as susceptible depletion limits further transmission.

## Implementation Notes

- Coefficients are inferred from training data and represent empirical fits
- Model is deterministic and pointwise (no temporal coupling)
- Each call independently predicts dI_dt from a single observation
- No smoothing, filtering, or trajectory information is used
- Coefficients are valid only for populations of ~1000; generalization to different scales untested
