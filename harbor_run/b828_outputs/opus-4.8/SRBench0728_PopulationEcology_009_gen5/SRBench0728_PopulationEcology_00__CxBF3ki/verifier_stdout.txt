"""
Discovered law for the observed dynamical system.

Target:  dN_dt  (instantaneous right-hand side of the population equation)
Inputs :  t, N, crowding_load   (only N and crowding_load are used)

Model (distributed-delay / crowding logistic):

    dN/dt = r * N * (1 - crowding_load / K)

where `crowding_load` is a lagged measure of the population that limits growth
(its own dynamics obey  d(crowding_load)/dt = 0.2 * (N - crowding_load),
i.e. an exponential moving average of N with time constant 5).

Per-capita growth  (1/N) dN/dt  falls linearly with the crowding load, is
positive when crowding_load < K, negative when it exceeds K, and vanishes at
the equilibrium N = crowding_load = K.  The coefficients were estimated from
the settling (near-equilibrium) portion of the trajectory, which is the regime
the hidden right-hand test segment lives in.
"""

# Fitted constants (estimated from the training trajectory, t >= 15)
R = 0.3605      # intrinsic per-capita growth rate
K = 902.22      # crowding-load carrying capacity (equilibrium level)


def law(input_data: list[dict[str, float]]) -> list[dict[str, float]]:
    out = []
    for row in input_data:
        N = row["N"]
        C = row["crowding_load"]
        dN_dt = R * N * (1.0 - C / K)
        out.append({"dN_dt": dN_dt})
    return out

# Discovered law for `dN_dt`

## Result

$$\boxed{\;\frac{dN}{dt} \;=\; r\,N\left(1-\frac{\text{crowding\_load}}{K}\right)\;}$$

with fitted constants

| constant | value | meaning |
|----------|-------|---------|
| `r` | **0.3605** | intrinsic per-capita growth rate |
| `K` | **902.22** | crowding-load carrying capacity (equilibrium level) |

Only `N` and `crowding_load` enter the law; `t` is not needed.

## Reading of the system

The three columns form a coupled dynamical system. I first characterised the
auxiliary variable and found an *exact* clean relation:

```
d(crowding_load)/dt = 0.2 * (N - crowding_load)     (R² = 0.99999999)
```

i.e. `crowding_load` is an **exponential moving average of the population `N`
with time constant τ = 5**. It is therefore a *lagged crowding signal*: it
tracks `N` but with delay.

The population itself grows logistically, but the density that limits growth is
this lagged crowding signal rather than the instantaneous `N`. This is the
classic **distributed-delay (Hutchinson-type) logistic model**, which naturally
produces the damped oscillations seen in the data:

```
dN/dt = r · N · (1 − crowding_load / K)
```

* Per-capita growth `(1/N)·dN/dt` decreases with the crowding load.
* Growth is positive while `crowding_load < K`, negative once it overshoots `K`,
  and the system settles at the fixed point `N = crowding_load = K ≈ 902`.

## Methodology

1. **Loaded** `train_data.csv` (4 500 rows, t ∈ [0, 54]).
2. Verified `dN_dt` equals the numerical derivative of `N` (clean, essentially
   noiseless data) and discovered `d(crowding_load)/dt = 0.2·(N − crowding_load)`.
3. **Screened functional forms** for `dN_dt` by non-linear least squares:
   logistic in `N`, logistic in `crowding_load`, Ricker/exponential crowding,
   Gompertz (`ln K/C`), power-law nullcline (`C ∝ N^p`), `(N−C)` couplings,
   and full polynomial surfaces in `(N, crowding_load)`.
   The crowding-logistic `r·N·(1 − C/K)` is the simplest form that reproduces
   the observed per-capita behaviour and the qualitative dynamics.
4. **Parameter estimation for extrapolation.** Because the hidden test set is
   the *right-hand time segment*, the trajectory there sits near the
   equilibrium the system is settling into. I therefore fit `r` and `K` on the
   settling portion of the training data (t ≥ 15), which is the regime that
   governs the extrapolation, giving `r = 0.3605`, `K = 902.22`. This choice was
   validated by holding out t ≥ 48 and fitting on t ∈ [15, 48): the
   near-equilibrium fit extrapolates forward better than a fit dominated by the
   large early transient.

## Honest limitations

The data is generated by a system whose full state is **higher-dimensional than
the two observed variables**: near equilibrium there exist pairs of samples with
essentially identical `(N, crowding_load)` but different `dN_dt` (differing by up
to ~4). A strict two-variable pointwise map therefore cannot be exact — an
unobserved (further-lagged) mode contributes a small residual oscillation
(amplitude a few units) that no function of `(t, N, crowding_load)` reproduced
robustly.

Within that constraint, `r·N·(1 − crowding_load/K)` is:
* the most accurate **interpretable** pointwise law found (global R² ≈ 0.93–0.96
  depending on the fit window), and
* the **best forward extrapolator** among all tested forms (adding `(N−C)`,
  quadratic, cubic, Gompertz or explicit-`t` correction terms did **not** improve
  held-out forward performance and often hurt it).

It captures the dominant crowding-limited growth and the correct equilibrium,
which is what dominates the settling right-hand test segment.
