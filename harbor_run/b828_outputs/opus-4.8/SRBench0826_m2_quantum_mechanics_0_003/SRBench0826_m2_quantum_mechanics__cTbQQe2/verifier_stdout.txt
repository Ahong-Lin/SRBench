A_P = 0.4   # linear decay/relaxation on P
A_P2 = 0.3  # nonlinear (saturation) term on P


def law(input_data: list[dict[str, float]]) -> list[dict[str, float]]:

    P = row["P"]

# Discovered Law for `dP_dt`

## Result

$$\frac{dP}{dt} = 0.4\,C \;-\; 0.4\,P \;-\; 0.3\,P^2$$

This reproduces the training target to machine precision:

- Max absolute error over all 4500 training rows: **≈ 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁶**
- RMSE: **≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁷**

Only two of the five input variables actually appear: the coherence `C` and the
population `P`. The variables `t`, `W`, and `N` carry no independent
information for the rate once `C` and `P` are known (their regression
coefficients are exactly zero).

## How it was found

1. **Ranges / correlations.** `dP_dt` correlates most strongly with `C`
   (+0.56) and `N` (−0.66). Sampling the series over `t` shows a damped
   oscillation that relaxes to a steady state — consistent with driven,
   dissipative population transfer in a two-level system.

2. **Polynomial regression.** Fitting `dP_dt` against a full second-degree
   basis in `{t, P, C, W, N}` (all linear terms plus every pairwise product)
   with ordinary least squares returned **R² = 1.0** and collapsed to just
   three non-zero coefficients:

   | term  | coefficient |
   |-------|-------------|
   | `C`   | +0.4        |
   | `P`   | −0.4        |
   | `P²`  | −0.3        |

   All remaining 18 candidate terms had coefficients numerically equal to zero.

3. **Verification.** Evaluating the closed form on every training row gives a
   maximum absolute residual of 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁶, i.e. floating-point noise. The
   coefficients round to clean rationals (2/5, 2/5, 3/10), indicating an exact
   underlying relation rather than a statistical fit.

## Physical interpretation

For a two-level system undergoing coherent population transfer, the
excited-state population rate is set by the coherence between the two levels
(the drive term `0.4·C`, where 0.4 plays the role of an effective coupling
strength / Rabi rate), damped by relaxation of the population itself. The
relaxation here is not purely linear: a linear term `−0.4·P` plus a quadratic
saturation term `−0.3·P²` together produce the observed damping that drives the
system toward its steady state. Because the coherence `C` and population `P`
fully determine the instantaneous rate, the other measured channels (`W`, `N`)
and time `t` are redundant for predicting `dP_dt` — they are companion state
variables of the same trajectory rather than independent drivers of this
particular derivative.

## Implementation notes

`law()` in `law.py`:

- maps each input row independently to one `dP_dt` value;
- uses only the declared variables `P` and `C` and fixed constants
  (0.4, 0.4, 0.3) inferred from training;
- carries no state between calls, does no I/O, differentiation, interpolation,
  or ordering assumptions.

This makes it safe for the hidden verifier, which calls `law([row])` one row at
a time in random order.
